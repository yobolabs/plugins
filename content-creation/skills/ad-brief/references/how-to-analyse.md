# How to analyse a video ad (the thinking, not the form)

Back to [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). The sections in SKILL.md say WHAT to write. This file says HOW an experienced
creative strategist thinks while watching, so the answers are insight, not a description of the frames.

## 0 · Watch it three times, for three different things

1. **As the viewer, at full speed, sound on.** Note only what you felt and remembered after one pass, the moment
   you would have scrolled past, and the moment that made you stay. That is the ad's real effect.
2. **As the editor, frame by frame.** Shot lengths, where the cuts fall against the music, what changes between
   shots (subject, colour, scale, angle), when text appears and leaves.
3. **As the maker, scene by scene.** For each scene ask: why is THIS shot here, and what would break if it were
   removed? A scene you can remove without loss had no purpose; say so.

## 1 · Start from the job, not the pictures

Every choice in an ad serves one job. Name it before anything else, because it explains everything after it:

| Signal | Usually means |
|---|---|
| Account is a brand, offer or price on screen, CTA, link | conversion or launch: the ad sells a decision |
| Account is a creator, "until they hire me", "concept", no CTA | spec or portfolio: the ad sells the maker's skill; craft and surprise matter more than the offer |
| Agency or production credit, polished multi-location shoot | brand campaign: desire and memorability over information |
| Phone look, a real person talking, comments-style captions | UGC or creator-led: trust and relatability over polish |

Say who it is for and where it runs (feed, story, reel; sound on or off). A reel built for sound-off viewing is a
different object from one built on its music.

## 2 · The hook and the hold

- **Hook (0–2 s):** what stops the thumb? A pattern interrupt, a question, a person looking at camera, an
  unexpected object, a bold caption, motion toward the lens. Name the mechanism, not just the content.
- **Hold:** what keeps them to the end? Rhythm (new image on every beat), curiosity (what comes next), escalation,
  variety. Measure it: shot count, average shot length, how many cuts sit on the beat.
- **Payoff:** what does the last second leave behind? The product, a logo, a line, a feeling.

## 3 · The message architecture

Find the ONE idea. Everything else is proof or decoration. Common architectures: problem → solution; before →
after; one product shown many ways (variety); one moment of use (ritual); social proof; a challenge or trend
format; pure aesthetic desire. Name the architecture and the one idea it carries; the style references in
`content-creation:higgsfield-video` `references/styles/` describe common types.

## 4 · How it looks, and why

For each visual choice, give the reason it was made:

- **Product scale and position:** hero (fills the frame), in context (used by a person), or in a world (small in a
  place). Scale says what the ad is about.
- **Colour:** one palette or a colour per shot? Matched to the product, or contrasting to make it pop?
- **Light and finish:** studio gloss, natural daylight, flash, phone. It signals premium vs real.
- **People:** faces (relatability, aspiration), hands only (the product stays the star), or none.
- **Composition devices:** stacks, fans, pairs, symmetry, macro texture, motion toward the lens, props that echo the
  product's shape or colour.
- **Text:** what it says, when, how long, and whether it carries the message or only labels.

## 5 · How it is assembled

Structure (one story, a montage, a reel of separate mini-ads, a demo, a talking head), the order of scenes and why
that order (build-up, variety, ending on the strongest image), transitions (hard cuts on the beat, match cuts,
whip pans), and the role of sound (music sets the pace; voice carries the message; SFX sells the texture).

## 6 · Separate what transfers from what doesn't

This is the part a remix depends on. For every scene write:

- **Transferable:** the job of the shot (its purpose), its framing, timing, energy and composition device.
- **Category-bound:** the places, props and actions that only make sense for the old product (applying a cream
  to skin, a sauce in a kitchen, a protein bar in a gym bag). These must change for the new product.
- **Brand-bound:** logos, packaging, signature colours, names. These must go.

## 7 · What made it work — be specific and testable

Not "good visuals". Instead: "the product fills 60–80% of every frame", "a new colour background on every beat",
"every still lasts under one second and lands on the beat", "the caption frames the whole video as a challenge". A remix can
check itself against a specific claim; it cannot check against praise.

## 8 · Honesty

- Evidence for every claim (a time, a frame, the caption, the account). No evidence → "unverified".
- Separate what you saw from what you infer ("the account is a creator" is seen; "they want to be hired by beauty
  brands" is inferred from the caption).
- If the ad does something you cannot explain, say so; do not invent a strategy to fill the gap.
